# Early Family Arrangements — Future Design Note

**Review status.** Ages 12–13 are a research question. Stage B ships adult marriage only. The table distinguishes statements in sources from historical interpretation, possible stories and parameters that still lack evidence.

| Arrangement | Direct evidence and limits | Interpretation and research question | Possible scene / unverified assumption |
|---|---|---|---|
| Family-arranged betrothal | The *Han shu* 97A account of Empress Zhang describes a match arranged by Empress Lü within the imperial family in Emperor Hui’s reign. It records her installation. It does not date a separate betrothal for a commoner child. [1] | Determine what promises, parties, witnesses and termination rights are actually attested for the target region and rank. | Two families discuss a future match. Store a proposal and its participants. An enforceable promise at age 12–13 remains unverified. |
| Early marriage | *Han shu* 97A explicitly gives the very young age of Empress Shangguan at installation under Emperor Zhao. This is a court office and dynastic alliance at Chang’an. [1] | Court precedent gives no population rate or minimum age for ordinary households under Emperor Wu. Check registration evidence for a dated, socially comparable case. | An exceptional elite childhood alliance could change household authority. The age, legal effects and applicability to other strata require separate review. |
| Dependent work or service | Transmitted biographies attest to labour for others. Ma’s study assembles occupational and geographic cases. A case in the *Hou Han shu* describes the orphan Cheng Gong herding pigs for someone else in his youth. The account concerns a different setting from a Wudi village. [2] | Distinguish employment, kin maintenance, guardianship and ownership. Herding for another household alone proves neither co-residence nor a transfer of legal status. | A relative offers board in return for work. Who may consent, receive earnings, end the arrangement or return home remains unverified. |
| Debt-driven separation | Chao Cuo’s memorial to Emperor Wen, transmitted in *Han shu* 24A, describes rural families selling property or descendants to meet debts. A policy argument supplies neither a household contract nor a frequency. [3] | Establish who owed the debt, who received payment, whether the child moved, and what status the transaction actually conferred. | A child goes to stay with kin after household assets are exhausted. Kin support, debt settlement and relocation require separate records. Ages and terms are fictional. |
| Sale and possible loss of freedom | *Han shu* 24A records Gaozu permitting child sales during exceptional famine and mentions Shu and Han as destinations for obtaining food. It gives no individual buyer, child age, detailed status or release terms. [3] | Require legal evidence for the resulting status, enforceable restrictions and release procedure in the selected period. Use the excavated legal texts and their critical edition to check vocabulary. [4] | Only a separately reviewed event could establish loss of freedom. A sale would foreclose ordinary marriage recruitment and change control of work, movement and earnings. No reward or romantic route attaches to it. |

**Proposed vertical slice for later review.** Start with a fixed, authored temporary stay with a named relative during hardship. Record birth kin, actual residence, guardian and scope of authority independently. Record the child's response, who decided, work obligations, who controls earnings, contact with home, and a dated return or review. Preserve person IDs, birth parentage and account ownership across departure and return. Exercise refusal by the receiving household and an interrupted return after a guardian dies. The scene can show missing a sibling or having little say about leaving. It creates no spouse, new estate, inheritance transfer or change in legal freedom.

A later betrothal would need a separately revocable arrangement with each party's authority, knowledge and response recorded. Any later loss-of-freedom slice would additionally need dated legal status with provenance, a controlling party, movement and labour restrictions, command eligibility, and a verified path for release or changed circumstances. Require appropriate evidence before enabling either transition. The simulator must never convert placement, debt or servitude into a spouse relationship.
